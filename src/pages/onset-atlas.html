How it works

Onsets split the phrase into events. Similar events merge into families, which become the graph's nodes; observed successions become its edges. Playing the graph walks it, taking each node's exits in turn.

The point of this page is the number in bold. The order period is measured by actually simulating the traversal, and it is often smaller than the number of events you recorded — sing "da da dee da" and two families with three edges give you a three-event loop, not a four-event one. A previous design in this line displayed 24 while the audible order was 3, because it counted alternate takes of the same syllable as events. This page refuses to do that, and warns you when the graph would give back less than you put in.

Distinctness is the honest control: raise it and fewer events merge, so the graph keeps more of your phrase. The tension is real — repetition creates branching but also shrinks the graph.
