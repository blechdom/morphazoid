# Domain evidence and claims

Read this only when an instrument is based on a real physical, biological,
historical, or cultural subject.

Inspect the closest existing research ledger before choosing a format. Keep a
compact mapping ledger:

| Claim or mechanism | Source and evidence strength | Implementation mapping | Simplification or uncertainty | Safe product wording |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| | | | | |

Distinguish:

- directly measured or documented mechanisms;
- supported generalizations;
- bounded engineering approximations;
- artistic mappings added for playability;
- explicit non-claims.

Prefer authoritative or primary sources. Use evidence-backed units and ranges
where they exist, then document any clamping or rescaling needed for a safe
browser instrument. Do not let a realistic label disguise an unrelated generic
oscillator, drone, noise bed, or animation.

For named living traditions, prefer practitioner, cultural-institution, or
specialist sources. Do not describe presets as authentic, canonical, complete,
or representative of an entire tradition without evidence and appropriate
review. Label comparisons and experimental extensions honestly.

Treat third-party recordings as listening references unless their license and
permitted use are recorded. Add required attribution and license notices before
shipping recordings, images, data, or code.

Keep detailed evidence in the research ledger. Catalogue and interface copy
should be concise, factual, and limited to claims the implementation supports.
