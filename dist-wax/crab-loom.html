How it works

A Möbius band is a loop plus an involution — any transform that undoes itself when applied twice. That is exactly why an odd-twist band takes two laps to return, and it is the same structure as a crab canon, where a line sounds against its own retrograde. Bach's Crab Canon from the Musical Offering is conventionally realised as a physical half-twisted strip.

Every option in the Involution menu squares to the identity, so each one is a legitimate Möbius. Track-lane swap is what real endless-loop cartridges did to double playing time.

Canon ↔ hocket crossfades between the two heads sounding together and strictly alternating. Hocket is one line distributed across voices so it seems like two; a Möbius band is one surface that seems like two. Same idea, different medium.

Cutting is the counter-intuitive part and the results here are the real topology: a centre cut through a one-sided band gives one band of double length, and an off-centre cut gives two linked bands of different lengths — a ratio you did not choose.

Honesty: polarity flip and inversion are audible stand-ins, not magnetic or pitch-domain models. The topology and period arithmetic are exact; the tape physics is a musical approximation.
