# MIDI artwork and artist collection — checked October 12, 2026

The collection adds **22 complete files**: all 19 decorative scores from the
already bundled BLEEDING EDGE MONOTONE archive, and three files from Hans5958's
own repository. No notes were deleted or simplified. Gzip decompresses to the
original MIDI bytes. The additions occupy **57,110,489 bytes compressed**;
`archives/decorative-validation-20261012.json` records sizes, SHA-256 hashes,
counts, duration, channels, tracks, and embedded credits.

These entries use `compression: "gzip"` and `visualOnly: true` to distinguish the
dense visualization collection from ordinary song/mixer/stamp choices.
`pairedAudioId` points only to a separately supplied creator AUDIO edition of
the same work, when one exists. It does not promise sample-exact correspondence:
the originals retain their own event times, and some editions have different
final note times. All complete originals remain available in the unchanged
[35-file archive](archives/black-midi-archive-2026.7z).

### rin-w's reusable patterns

[Reusable Black MIDI Patterns and Phrases](https://github.com/rin-w/black-midi-patterns)
is a genuine reusable artwork collection with MIDI, Domino part and FL Studio
score formats. All nine `.mid` patterns are already bundled: block glisses,
funnels, lines and a volcanic web. The contribution terms allow unrestricted
use/modification of the patterns; the exact README is retained in
[BLACK-MIDI-PATTERNS.md](licenses/BLACK-MIDI-PATTERNS.md). These are short pieces
of artwork for reuse, not full song arrangements.

## Conlon Nancarrow: authentic holdings, not a free downloadable collection

The [Paul Sacher Foundation's MIDI Files of the Rolls](https://www.paul-sacher-stiftung.ch/en/research/1-2-Conlon-Nancarrow-Edition-project/6-midi-files-rolls.html)
is the strongest primary source. Its holdings include 280 original rolls and
MIDI derived from optical scans. Its research interface combines edited files
with the Nancarrow Sampler; the page describes access at the Foundation and
does not provide a public `.mid` collection download. This is a real archival
collection, but it is not included in our playable files.

[Kyle Gann's 2005 account](https://www.kylegann.com/PC050217-NancarrowSaga.html)
mentions a MIDI of “UK Finished A.” A functioning MIDI link was not verified
in the currently accessible page. The
[Robert Willey research article](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.14.20.1/mto.14.20.1.willey.php)
explains how scanned rolls and score-derived MIDI require decisions about
tempo, dynamics, articulation and instrument assignment; it is a technical
source rather than a free complete MIDI collection.

A [public archive entry for Nancarrow's Tango](https://www.alexanderpeppe.com/midi/010-2002-nancarrow-tango-sun-1abcf5df/)
identifies a Disklavier performance by Mei-Ting Sun from June 9, 2002.
It is one performed work, not the player-piano Studies collection or an original
roll scan; redistribution terms were not established here, so it is linked only.

No generated “Nancarrow-style” example is relabelled as Nancarrow, and no audio
preview is presented as a downloadable MIDI score.

## Counting and integrity

`archives/inspect-midi.py` checks `MThd`/`MTrk` boundaries and counts every
positive-velocity Note On event, including overlapping attacks and quiet art.
Zero-velocity Note On is a release, not an additional note. It follows the global
tempo map for note/file durations without allocating an object per note.
Counts measure MIDI attacks, not the voices a particular SoundFont can sustain.

For compressed entries, `sha256` and `bytes` describe the served gzip file;
`uncompressedSha256` and `uncompressedBytes` describe the untouched original
MIDI. Decompression and both hashes are checked independently for every entry.
The browser's supported rendering/audio limits remain runtime concerns; a
verified downloadable original is not by itself proof of real-time performance.
