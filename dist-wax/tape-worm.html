How it works / listening exercise

Try this first

Enable Audio and Play to hear the two demo phrases. Drag a round OUT gate to change where the worm leaves; drag a diamond IN gate to change where it arrives.

What you hear

One reader plays a single tape at a time. At the OUT gate it crossfades into the other tape's IN position. Tape A leans left; Tape B leans right.

What the picture means

The cream worm is the playback head. Colored contours show recorded amplitude. Round OUT gates are departures; diamond IN gates are arrivals. Dashed arrows show cross-tape routes, not editable graph edges.

The mechanism

Both tapes share the Leave at and Arrive at percentages. With splices bypassed, the current tape loops by itself—not both tapes together. Tape speed changes both duration and pitch. Moving a gate never edits the recording.

Controls

Tape speed
1× is original pitch and duration. 0.5× is twice as long and one octave lower; 2× is half as long and one octave higher.
Leave at
Shared departure position on both recordings, measured clockwise from the top. Drag either round OUT gate.
Arrive at
Shared arrival position in the other recording. Drag either diamond IN gate. If it is beyond OUT, the head wraps around before leaving.

Pause, reset, and memory

Choose Tape A/B before replacing it with a file or recording. Capture is limited to 12 seconds; Pause affects playback, not an active capture. Stop recording or Audio Off finishes capture. Reloading or leaving this page loses the recordings; there is no audio export yet.

Audio Off is mute, not Pause. It closes owned microphone tracks but retains transport. Monitor can pass live microphone audio while paused until you turn it off.

Keyboard

Focus the stage. Left/right selects A/B; Enter or 1–2 puts the reader at that tape's start. Up/down moves the shared OUT gates. Arrive at has its own slider. Space toggles Play.

What this demo does not do

Exactly two mono recordings and one reader. No added play heads, moving record heads, free graph layout, onset detection, or automatic graph construction.

About this starting instrument
