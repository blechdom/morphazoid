What the “square dance” really was

Partners exchange spin states—not places on a drawn square.

In the 2007 NIST experiment, about 60,000 rubidium-87 atoms were paired in optical-lattice double wells. Merging each pair created an exchange interaction between ground and excited vibrational modes. The measured population recurrence was 285(1) μs, with coherence lasting more than 10 ms.

  • Atoms do not trace square paths. “Square dance” is a metaphor for many partner pairs swapping spins in parallel across a lattice.
  • 50/50 populations alone do not prove entanglement. A dephased classical mixture can have the same populations; coherent relative phase is essential.
  • There is no faster-than-light signal. Neither partner’s random local result carries a controllable message.
  • This is a classical browser simulation. It is not live quantum hardware, QPU output, experimental data streaming, or audio emitted by atoms.

Quantum Square Dance classically simulates the ideal two-state exchange of one opposite-spin rubidium pair. The central double well represents the experiment’s merged ground and excited vibrational modes. The exchange phase moves the joint state from up-down through a maximally entangled square-root-of-SWAP, to a separable down-up full SWAP, through a second maximally entangled point, and back. A dim lattice represents many pairs exchanging in parallel. No atom follows a literal square trajectory.
