The active Shadertoy sound path is translated from GLSL to WGSL and rendered in bounded stereo chunks. Musically meaningful active-path values are editable; numerical stability constants, 12-TET math, BLEP calibration, sample rate, and the safety ceiling remain fixed. Dormant source experiments are intentionally omitted.

A WebGPU compute shader renders a procedural tracker composition with two upper pulse voices, bass, lead, arpeggio, noise percussion, drums, and ping-pong echo into Web Audio. Its live horizontal editor shows the loaded preset as one pattern ready for customization.

Six original code-drawn, low-resolution arcade invaders occupy separate performance bays and share the shader's master transport. The first five characters read only their own pitched voice sequence. The sixth reads the kick, snare, hats, and shaker sequences. Divisions of each real step position drive whole-body jumps and pulses, independent left and right arm waves, alternating leg kicks, eyes, expressions, accessories, and beat lamps. Level changes size and brightness; register and notes change height and color; waveform, pulse shape, articulation, and voice-specific timbre change each silhouette and movement. Slow grouped body phrases and faster limb accents stay on that same voice clock; there is no separate animation clock. Rests dim sound accents without snapping the slow pose. On the drum dancer, kick drives jumps and chest pulses, snare alternates the stick arms, hats drive feet and cymbal antennae, and shaker drives side-to-side movement. Echo makes afterimages, kick sends floor bands, snare throws blocks, and hats and shaker make edge sparks.

Upper A is a cyan sensor-crab invader performing precise angular freezes and scanner hits. Upper B is a lime beetle-runner invader gliding laterally with counterweighted fins. Bass is a purple horned boss invader using grounded contractions and heavy stomps. Lead is a pink crowned blade invader making long theatrical reaches and sword sweeps. Arp is an amber winged moth invader using suspended wing fans and orbiting turns. Drums is a red-orange beat robot with cymbal antennae, drumsticks, a pulsing kick-drum chest, and four independently driven limbs.

Click a character bay to select that performer and open its sequence editor. A click does not change effects. Drag at least a few pixels to change the two live effects instead. Horizontal movement controls the effect shown with a left-right arrow; vertical movement controls the effect shown with an up-down arrow. The center preserves the preset exactly, while the edges reach the parameter's correctly distributed low and high limits. With the character canvas focused, arrow keys adjust the selected bay, Shift plus arrows makes larger changes, Page Up and Page Down select another performer, and Home returns that bay to its neutral center. Press M to mute or unmute the selected voice and S to add or remove it from the solo group. Mute wins over Solo. These controls do not enable Audio or start or stop transport.

In Pattern Pad, drag horizontally to change the deterministic pattern seed and vertically to change pitch range. The right panel mirrors both axes with exact controls and reports how many custom voice edits are fixed in place. Unedited preset steps are reshaped live.

Click a performer to open its sequence and voice-specific sound controls. Pattern mode repeats independent editable loops; Song mode preserves the original arrangement and phrase behavior. Switching modes retains each sequence separately. Reloading a preset resets both. Loop length sets 1 to 32 active steps. Step fractions use one shared master unit, with independent fractions and loop durations for each voice. Compare all voice loop lengths in the right panel. Click a grid step to select it. Double-click, Enter, or Space turns it on or off. Drag vertically to change pitch; the Arp contour is pitch, not volume. The lower bars and Selected Step Volume slider control loudness separately. Drum height is 0–100 percent hit strength; each drum has its own mute and solo. Solo selects within the drum kit. Live drum edits, and pitched edits in Pattern mode, wait until the edited step's next occurrence; they never trigger preview notes or restart transport. X zoom fits all 32 stored steps or enlarges 16 or 8 steps. Use X pan or page buttons to reach the other steps. Dimmed steps are stored outside the active loop. Y pan and Visible Pitch Range adjust the pitch view. Left and right select steps; up and down change values; Shift changes pitched lanes by an octave. Page Up and Page Down select voices. A or Delete resets a step, N or H enables it, and R or O writes silence. In Song mode, the white Lead and Arp traces show the extra phrase-driven pitch; these traces are absent in Pattern mode.
