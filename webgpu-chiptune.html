The active Shadertoy sound path is translated from GLSL to WGSL and rendered in bounded stereo chunks. Musically meaningful active-path values are editable; numerical stability constants, 12-TET math, BLEP calibration, sample rate, and the safety ceiling remain fixed. Dormant source experiments are intentionally omitted.

A WebGPU compute shader renders a procedural tracker composition with two upper pulse voices, bass, lead, arpeggio, noise percussion, drums, and ping-pong echo into Web Audio. Its live horizontal editor keeps the original procedural source available in every cell.

Five original code-drawn, low-resolution arcade invaders occupy separate voice bays and share the shader's master transport. Their formation steps, feet, claws, wings, and beat lamps follow each voice's sequence clock and current step. Level changes size and brightness; register and notes change height and color; waveform, pulse shape, articulation, and voice-specific timbre change each silhouette and movement. Slow grouped body phrases stay on each voice clock while rapid notes appear as lamps and small accents. Rests dim those sound accents without snapping the body to a neutral pose. Echo makes afterimages, kick sends floor bands, snare throws blocks, and hats and shaker make edge sparks.

Upper A is a cyan sensor-crab invader performing precise angular freezes and scanner hits. Upper B is a lime beetle-runner invader gliding laterally with counterweighted fins. Bass is a purple horned boss invader using grounded contractions and heavy stomps. Lead is a pink crowned blade invader making long theatrical reaches and sword sweeps. Arp is an amber winged moth invader using suspended wing fans and orbiting turns.

In Source Pad, drag horizontally to change the deterministic pattern seed and vertically to change pitch range. The right panel mirrors both axes with exact controls and reports how many active cells still use SOURCE. Hand-drawn notes are intentionally not regenerated.

In Sequence, the canvas devotes its full height to one selected lane. Choose SOURCE, NOTE or POINT, REST, HIT, or OFF, then drag across the large cells. Vertical position sets note or shape value and horizontal motion paints every crossed step. Drums are binary. The mini tracker below shows all nine independent lane playheads and selects lanes or steps. FOLLOW NEXT keeps the inspector on the first safely audible step; locking a distant step shows a truthful countdown. Left and right select steps; up and down change values; Shift changes pitched lanes by an octave. Page Up and Page Down select lanes. Enter or Space cycles a cell. A restores SOURCE, N or H writes a note or hit, R or O writes a rest or off, and Delete restores SOURCE. Every lane derives from one master clock while its fractional rate, phase, and loop length determine its local playhead.
